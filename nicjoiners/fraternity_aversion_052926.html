Key finding
Drug use and hazing are the primary behavioral barriers. Concern levels for both are more than double among non-joiners compared to joiners.
Cost gap
Cost of joining shows the smallest mean difference (+0.34). All behavioral concerns - drug use, hazing, stigma, stereotype - show larger gaps than any cost item.
Time and academic concerns
Time commitment and academic impact show moderate gaps (+0.41 and +0.52). Both are elevated among non-joiners but score below the midpoint of the 1-to-4 scale for that group.
03

Who Scores High on Aversion?

Each tab shows a different demographic or background variable and its relationship to the aversion composite. The pattern shifts meaningfully across all seven tabs, and no single variable tells the whole story.

Mean aversion score by political views

Monotonic relationship across the full spectrum. The 1.74-unit spread from very conservative (-0.45) to very liberal (+1.29) is the single strongest demographic predictor of aversion in this dataset.

Political orientation predicts aversion more strongly than any other demographic variable. Conservative respondents score below the mean, moderates cluster near zero, and liberal respondents score above. The relationship holds at every step across all five categories.

Mean aversion score by race/ethnicity

All non-White groups score above the overall mean. Asian and Asian American respondents show the highest aversion (+0.74). Non-Hispanic White respondents are the only group below zero.

Aversion is higher among all non-White racial groups relative to Non-Hispanic White respondents. The survey does not capture why these differences exist; further qualitative research would be needed to interpret the drivers.

Mean aversion score by religious identity

Self-identified religious respondents average near zero (-0.05). Secular and spiritual-but-not-religious respondents are substantially higher, consistent with the conservative/religious cluster being more fraternity-oriented.

Students who identify as religious score substantially lower on aversion than those who identify as secular or spiritual-but-not-religious. This aligns with the PCA finding that conservative political identity and formal religious affiliation load together on the same component.

Mean aversion score by HS fraternity interest

Strong gradient. Those "not interested" in high school average +1.05; they arrived with both low intent and high concern. "Very interested" averages -0.85. Pre-college interest and concern level co-travel as a single orientation.

Pre-college interest in fraternities and aversion co-move across the full range of the scale. The PCA confirmed these two variables load on the same component rather than operating independently.

Mean aversion score: HS sports vs. music participation
HS Sports
HS Music

Four-year athletes average -0.11; four-year music participants average +0.61. These two activities define contrasting social orientations toward Greek life, not simply different hobbies.

Aversion scores move in opposite directions across years of athletic versus music participation. The gradient is consistent at every participation level for both activities.

Mean aversion score by school choice motivation
Importance of Greek Life
Importance of Academics

Choosing school for Greek life strongly predicts low aversion (-0.59 for "very important"). Academic reputation as primary driver predicts higher aversion (+0.25).

School selection motivation and aversion scores move together consistently across all response categories. Students who rated Greek life as very important average -0.59; students who rated it as not at all important average +0.26.

Mean aversion score: financial indicators

Pell Grant recipients score +0.38 vs -0.01 for non-recipients. Financial constraint and aversion are linked, but this is a secondary driver compared to politics, race, and HS interest.

Pell Grant recipients and first-generation students both show elevated aversion scores. Both gaps are statistically significant but smaller in magnitude than the gaps observed for political orientation, race, and HS interest.

04

What the PCA Revealed

All 11 concern variables collapse onto one principal component. In the predictive model they were counted as 11 separate predictors accounting for 33% of total importance. The PCA shows they share a single underlying dimension.

Single dimension
All 11 concern variables load positively onto PC1. There is no evidence of distinct sub-factors separating behavioral concerns from cost or time concerns - they co-vary as one attitude.
PC1 loadings (all positive)
Time commitment (0.75), academic impact (0.73), too many commitments (0.73), stigma (0.68), hazing (0.66), drug use (0.63), not fitting stereotype (0.63), frat house (0.56), cost to participate (0.43), cost to join (0.42), not top choice (0.38).
Recommendation
Replace all 11 concern variables with this single composite in future models. Reduces the feature set while retaining predictive power and produces a more interpretable output for NIC stakeholders.
PC1 loadings: contribution of each concern to the aversion composite

Time commitment, academic impact, and too many commitments carry the highest loadings, though all 11 items contribute meaningfully. The lowest loading (not top choice, 0.38) still represents a substantial positive contribution.
