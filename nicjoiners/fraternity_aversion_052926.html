Score distribution

Non-joiner scores are strongly right-skewed. Joiner scores cluster near zero and below. The distributions overlap in the mid-range.

Non-joiners average a composite score of +2.57. Joiners average −0.01. Eighty-one percent of non-joiners score above the joiner median. The effect size is Cohen's d = 0.96, which is large by conventional standards. Despite the separation, the overlap is substantial enough that aversion score alone cannot reliably classify individuals.

Drug use (+1.10 gap) and hazing (+0.94) are the sharpest dividers. Cost of joining has the smallest gap (+0.34).

Non-joiners score higher than joiners on every concern item. Drug and alcohol use shows the largest mean difference (joiners 1.39, non-joiners 2.49). Hazing is second (joiners 1.60, non-joiners 2.54). Cost of joining shows the smallest difference (joiners 2.13, non-joiners 2.47), meaning cost concern is relatively similar across both groups.

Concern Item Breakdown

Concern Joiners Non-joiners Gap Magnitude

All items scored 1 (not at all concerned) to 4 (very concerned). Sorted by gap size descending.

The top four concern items by gap size are drug/alcohol use, hazing, living in a fraternity house, and not fitting the stereotype. These four items all have larger gaps than any of the cost or time-commitment items, which are elevated for both groups. All 11 items load onto a single principal component in the PCA, confirming they measure one underlying attitude rather than distinct concerns.

Key finding
Drug use and hazing are the primary behavioral barriers. These concern levels are more than double for non-joiners compared to joiners.
Cost gap
Cost of joining shows the smallest mean difference between joiners and non-joiners (+0.34). All behavioral concerns (drug use, hazing, stigma, stereotype) show larger gaps than any cost item.
Time and academic concerns
Time commitment and academic impact show moderate gaps (+0.41 and +0.52). Both are elevated among non-joiners but score below the midpoint of the 1-to-4 scale for that group.
03

Who Scores High on Aversion?

Each tab below shows a different demographic or background variable and its relationship to the aversion composite score. Click through all seven tabs. The pattern shifts meaningfully across each one, and no single variable tells the whole story.

Mean aversion score by political views

Monotonic relationship across the full spectrum. The 1.74-unit spread from very conservative (−0.45) to very liberal (+1.29) is the single strongest demographic predictor of aversion.

Political orientation is the strongest demographic predictor of aversion in this dataset. The relationship is monotonic across all five categories: conservative respondents score below the mean, moderate respondents cluster near zero, and liberal respondents score above. The 1.74-unit spread between the two endpoints is larger than any other single variable in the analysis.

Mean aversion score by race/ethnicity

All non-White groups score above the overall mean. Asian and Asian American respondents show the highest aversion (+0.74). Non-Hispanic White respondents are the only group below zero.

Aversion is higher among all non-White racial groups relative to Non-Hispanic White respondents. Asian and Asian American students show the highest mean score (+0.74). Non-Hispanic White respondents are the only group below zero. The survey does not capture why these differences exist; further qualitative research would be needed to interpret the drivers.

Mean aversion score by religious identity

Self-identified religious respondents average near zero (−0.05). Secular and spiritual-but-not-religious respondents are substantially higher. Consistent with the conservative/religious cluster being more fraternity-oriented.

Students who identify as religious score substantially lower on aversion than those who identify as secular or spiritual-but-not-religious. This is consistent with the PCA finding that conservative political identity and formal religious affiliation load together on the same component.

Mean aversion score by HS fraternity interest

Strong gradient. Those "not interested" in HS average +1.05; they arrived with both low intent and high concern. "Very interested" averages −0.85. Pre-college interest and concern level co-travel as a single orientation, not independent inputs.

Pre-college interest in fraternities and aversion co-move across the full range of the scale. Students who were actively disinterested in high school arrived on campus with both lower intent and higher concern scores. Students who were very interested arrived with both high motivation and low concern scores. The PCA confirmed these two variables load on the same component rather than operating independently.

Mean aversion score: HS sports vs. music participation
HS Sports (never → all 4 years) HS Music (never → all 4 years)

These two activities define contrasting cultural tracks. Four-year athletes average −0.11; four-year music participants average +0.61. Not just different hobbies, but different social orientations toward Greek life.

Aversion scores move in opposite directions across years of athletic versus music participation. Four-year athletes average the lowest aversion scores in the dataset (−0.11). Four-year music participants average among the highest (+0.61). The gradient is consistent across participation levels for both activities.

Mean aversion score by school choice motivation

Choosing school for Greek life strongly predicts low aversion (−0.59 for "very important"). Academic reputation as the primary driver predicts higher aversion (+0.25); these students chose schools where fraternity culture is secondary.

School selection motivation is strongly associated with aversion scores. Students who rated Greek life as very important when choosing their school average −0.59. Students who rated academic reputation as very important average +0.25. Students who rated Greek life as not at all important average +0.26. The school choice decision and the aversion score move together consistently across all response categories.

Mean aversion score: financial indicators

Pell Grant recipients score +0.38 vs −0.01 for non-recipients. Financial constraint and aversion are linked, but this is a secondary driver compared to politics, race, and HS interest.

Pell Grant recipients average an aversion score of +0.38, compared to −0.01 for non-recipients. First-generation students average +0.17, compared to +0.02 for non-first-generation students. Both gaps are statistically significant but smaller in magnitude than the gaps observed for political orientation, race, and HS interest.

04

What the PCA Revealed

PC2: General Fraternity Aversion
All 11 concern variables collapse onto one principal component (PC2). In the predictive model they were counted as 11 separate predictors accounting for 33% of total importance. The PCA shows they share a single underlying dimension.
PC2 loadings (all positive)
Time commitment (0.75), academic impact (0.73), too many commitments (0.73), stigma (0.68), hazing (0.66), drug use (0.63), not fitting stereotype (0.63), frat house (0.56), cost to participate (0.43), cost to join (0.42), not top choice (0.38).
Recommendation
Replace all 11 concern variables with this single composite score in future models. Reduces feature set while retaining predictive power, and produces a more interpretable output for NIC stakeholders.
