Component Structure

Ten components were extracted from 78 candidate predictor variables using varimax rotation. Each card below shows the variables loading on that component, their loadings, and a note on how to interpret the grouping. Components with dashed left borders have mixed or negative signs.

PC1: Parental Education / First-Gen
2 variables · loadings 0.89-0.90
Highest parental education-0.90
First-gen status-0.89
Near-perfect redundancy. These two variables measure the same thing. Use one.

Students whose parents did not attend college are systematically less likely to join a fraternity. Parental education and first-generation status load almost identically (0.90 and 0.89), so only one variable is needed in any subsequent model.

All 11 concern items move together as one underlying attitude. A student who reports concern about hazing almost certainly also reports concern about drug use, stigma, time commitment, and not fitting in. Addressing any single concern in isolation is unlikely to shift the overall barrier, which is a holistic skepticism toward fraternity culture.

Age and class year should not both be included in any model - they carry the same information in this dataset. The component also weakly picks up transfer status, indicating that students further along in their college career differ modestly in their joining behavior from first-year students.

Implication - Modeling
The 11 concern items in PC2 should be reduced to a single composite (aversion score) in any predictive model. Treating them as 11 separate predictors inflates their apparent importance relative to other variable groups.
Implication - Variable Selection
PC1 and PC4 each contain near-perfectly redundant pairs. Dropping one from each pair (first-gen or parental education; age or class year) reduces multicollinearity with no loss of signal.
Implication - Standalone Predictors
Race/ethnicity, individual sport indicators, and several funding variables do not group with any component. They carry independent signal and should remain as separate terms in any model.
