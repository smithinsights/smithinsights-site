02

Two Distinct Attrition Profiles

The withdrawal data reveals two mechanically different problems. Clusters with high pre-OHP attrition are losing PNMs before any recruitment interaction happens - that is a communication and expectation-setting failure. Clusters with high post-OHP attrition are losing PNMs at schedule release - that is a schedule disappointment and RC coaching failure. Coaching interventions must be matched to the profile, not applied uniformly.

Profile A: Pre-OHP Attrition (C2, C8, C9)
Campuses losing 20-29% of PNMs before open house. Phired Up attributes early-round withdrawal primarily to logistical conflicts, financial confusion, and process misunderstanding. These communities need pre-recruitment RC activation, financial transparency communication, and orientation redesign - all before Round 1 begins.
Profile B: Post-OHP Attrition (C4, C5, C7, C10)
Campuses holding PNMs through open house but losing them at schedule release. Phired Up identifies schedule dissatisfaction as 38% of all withdrawal rationale, peaking at Rounds 3-4. These communities need RC schedule-release coaching, pre-round expectation setting, and structured post-primary COB pathways.
Mixed Profile (C1, C3, C6)
These clusters show moderate attrition on both sides. C1 (PSR communities) has elevated pre-OHP withdrawal at 18.5% - closer to the Profile A clusters - despite having structured recruitment. C3 and C6 sit between both profiles and likely require campus-by-campus diagnosis rather than cluster-level programming.

05

Coaching Prioritization for the 2026-28 Cycle

Given finite coaching capacity, the Phired Up framework is most efficiently deployed by cluster profile rather than uniformly. The two attrition profiles point to two distinct coaching tracks, with a small group of clusters warranting individualized campus-level diagnosis.

Track 1: Pre-Recruitment Intervention (C2, C8, C9)
Pre-OHP withdrawal of 20-29% indicates PNMs disengaging before any meaningful recruitment experience. Focus: RC activation before Round 1, orientation redesign, financial transparency, and post-primary COB pathway planning. These communities cannot solve a pre-round problem with mid-round coaching.
Track 2: Schedule-Release Coaching (C4, C5, C7, C10)
Post-OHP withdrawal of 16-19% with relatively low pre-OHP rates indicates PNMs are engaged but vulnerable at schedule receipt. Focus: RC pre-coaching before Round 2 schedules drop, expectation normalization, and belonging-centered chapter conversation training. C4's 77% match rate shows what is achievable at scale with structural support.
Track 3: Campus-Level Diagnosis (C1, C3, C6)
C1 (PSR communities) shows elevated pre-OHP withdrawal despite structured recruitment - suggesting execution variability across its 68 campuses. C3 (staffing/context) campuses need governance assessment before recruitment coaching applies. C6 (regional/directional) campuses vary too widely in chapter count and OHP to generalize. Area Advisor coaching briefs are the right vehicle here.
