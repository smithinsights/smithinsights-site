Overview

This report is based on 102 former members who completed the resignation survey and were matched against the database, all coded Former Member. All are treated as voluntary resignations, including 2 coded Expelled - Discipline in the database, since the survey itself is the selection mechanism: only members who left described their own departure.

Respondents rated 23 possible reasons for leaving on a 1-5 scale from "Did not influence" to "Was the primary reason I left." An exploratory factor analysis on the 57 respondents with complete data across all 23 items (KMO = 0.656, Bartlett's chi-square = 526.0, p < 0.001) resolved four underlying factors, detailed below.

Key Finding

Hazing, bullying, and alcohol-related conflict do not form their own factor here. They load onto the same factor as Belonging and Values. Among members who already completed the membership process, chapter culture problems and outright toxic behavior are not experienced as separate issues. They are one and the same failure to belong.

Factor Structure

Four-factor varimax solution, complete cases only (n=57). Items shown are those loading at 0.40 or higher; Cronbach's alpha is computed on that same core item set per factor, not on every item that happened to load highest on that factor.

F1
Culture and Fit
alpha = 0.861  ·  17.5% of variance  ·  9 items
Values Belonging Hazing Experience gap Bullying Alcohol/partying Religion Mental health Conflict
Belonging failure, hazing, bullying, alcohol-related conflict, and value misalignment all load onto one factor rather than separating into distinct culture and toxic-environment dimensions. Initiated members appear not to compartmentalize a chapter's toxic behaviors from a broader sense of not belonging: the two collapse into a single lived experience of the chapter.
F2
Academic and Departure
alpha = 0.709  ·  9.5% of variance  ·  4 items
Academics (grades) Academics (major/time) Left school Alcohol/partying
Academic strain and dropping out of school entirely group together, with a secondary link to alcohol/partying. This factor reads as a distinct pathway out of the chapter driven by what is happening in the classroom, separate from culture or finances.
F3
Organizational Dysfunction
alpha = 0.602  ·  7.5% of variance  ·  3 items
Conflict Coach program Change in org
Conflict that felt mismanaged, a weak mentorship/coach program, and a disruptive change in the organization cluster together. Conflict also cross-loads onto Culture and Fit (loading 0.41), so mismanaged conflict reads as both an organizational failure and a belonging failure at once.
F4
Dependent Financial
alpha = 0.817  ·  7.0% of variance  ·  2 items
Financial (parents) Financial (debt)
Financial strain carried by parents and accumulated debt form a clean, tight factor, distinct from paying out of one's own pocket (which loads instead with the Culture and Fit factor). This is specifically about financial dependence on someone else running out, not personal cash flow.

Sampling adequacy for this solution is mediocre by conventional standards (KMO = 0.656; values above 0.80 are considered good) and the 57-case, 23-item ratio is thin relative to standard factor-analytic guidance of five to ten cases per item. The structure above should be read as a stable, recurring pattern across survey cycles, not as a precise or final model.

Regret Analysis

77 of 102 respondents answered the regret question. Comparing Yes vs. No groups with two-sided Mann-Whitney U tests, Belonging (p = 0.010) and Academics - major/time (p = 0.027) are the only items reaching conventional significance; Bullying (p = 0.082) and Rules (p = 0.085) are marginal.

Yes, I regret resigning
13.0%  ·  n=10
Median tenure: 10.8 months  ·  Belonging avg: 1.60  ·  Financial (own pocket) avg: 3.40

Left for reasons largely external to the chapter while still feeling connected to it. Low Belonging (p=0.010, Mann-Whitney) and high personal financial strain are what separate this group from non-regretters. Not one of the 10 regretters received a genuine solution offer before departing (8 said no, 2 said sort of).

No, I do not regret resigning
87.0%  ·  n=67
Median tenure: 17.7 months  ·  Belonging avg: 2.81  ·  Worth avg: 3.37

Longer median tenure and higher Belonging and Worth ratings describe a group that left because the chapter itself failed to deliver, a deliberate decision made after enough time to judge the experience fairly.

Methodology Notes

This section documents the full analytical pipeline in enough detail to reproduce or audit it later, independent of who runs the next cycle.

Sample and Data Provenance
  • Respondents: 102 unique survey respondents, merged across successive SurveyMonkey collection waves as the survey continues to be fielded. Each respondent's originating wave is retained in the master (Survey Wave column) for audit purposes.
  • Duplicate resolution: One respondent had two submissions under the same 18-character database ID, a known artifact of this survey platform. Rule applied: keep the more complete submission (more non-null answers), not automatically the earlier one by timestamp. In this instance the earlier submission was abandoned after two questions and the later one was the complete, usable response, so the later submission was kept. Earlier-wins is only the tie-break when completeness is roughly equal.
  • Re-export detection: Each new export is checked against the existing master by exact (Respondent ID, Start Date) match, not by database ID alone, since a legitimate retake can share a database ID with a prior row. Rows matching an existing (Respondent ID, Start Date) pair are treated as re-exports of already-captured data and are not re-added.
  • Cohort match: All 102 respondents matched to the member database on the 18-character ID and are coded Former Member. 6 have a blank Status Reason in the database, ranging from recently matched respondents back to one dating to 2019; these are treated as resigned on the logic that the survey itself is the selection mechanism, only members who left take it, but the blank coding is a database gap, not a resolved data point. 2 respondents are coded Expelled - Discipline and are folded in as resigned for the same reason. No respondent was unmatched, miscoded as an active or alumni member, or missing both a Resignation Date and a Current Status Begin Date (the two fields duration is calculated from).
  • Instrument change: The September 2026 export introduced 5 new influence items (Acceptance, Burnout, Culture, Departures, Overlooked) and one new open item on how long a member considered leaving before resigning. These are retained in the master going forward as the standing 28-item schema, but excluded from every analysis in this report: only a handful of the 102 respondents have been asked them so far, far too few to compute a stable mean, a factor loading, or a significance test. They will be incorporated once enough respondents have answered them, likely once complete-case n for the full 28-item set approaches the order of magnitude the current 23-item factor analysis is built on (n in the 50s).
Scoring
  • Scale: Five-point ordinal scale on 23 base items: 1 = Did not influence, 2 = Had a small influence, 3 = Somewhat influenced, 4 = Had a significant influence, 5 = Was the primary reason I left.
  • Missing data: No N/A option exists on this survey. A skipped item is coded as missing (NaN), never as zero or as the scale midpoint. Item-level sample sizes in the All Influence Ratings section therefore vary (n=65 to n=77) depending on how many respondents answered that specific item, and are computed independently per item rather than restricted to complete-case respondents.
Factor Analysis
  • Method: Exploratory factor analysis (principal-axis extraction) via Python's factor_analyzer 0.5.1, varimax (orthogonal) rotation, factors fixed at 4 to preserve comparability across report updates. The 4-factor count is a carried-forward assumption, not re-derived from a scree plot, parallel analysis, or eigenvalue criterion each time the report is regenerated; revisit this if the item scale changes materially (see Instrument change, above).
  • Sample: Complete cases only, i.e. respondents who answered all 23 base-scale items (listwise deletion). Complete-case n is 57.
  • Sampling adequacy: Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin measure = 0.656 (conventionally: below 0.60 unacceptable, 0.60-0.69 mediocre, 0.70-0.79 middling, 0.80+ good). Bartlett's test of sphericity: chi-square = 526.0, p < 0.001, meaning the item correlation matrix is significantly different from an identity matrix and factoring is defensible, despite the mediocre KMO.
  • Item retention per factor: An item is treated as belonging to a factor if its absolute loading on that factor is 0.40 or higher (a conventional salience threshold, not a data-driven cutoff). An item can cross-load onto more than one factor above this threshold (Conflict does, onto both Culture and Fit and Organizational Dysfunction) and is shown under both.
  • Reliability: Cronbach's alpha is computed only on each factor's retained (>=0.40 loading) item set, not on every item whose single highest loading happens to fall on that factor. This is a deliberately more conservative reliability estimate than a raw argmax assignment would give.
  • Variance explained: Reported per factor as that factor's eigenvalue-derived proportion of total variance across all 23 items post-rotation, not as a percentage of variance among only the items retained in that factor's core set.
Lifecycle Bucketing
  • Duration: months_initiated = (end_date - Initiation Date) in days, divided by 30.44. end_date is Resignation Date, falling back to Current Status Begin Date when Resignation Date is null.
  • Buckets: Y1 = 0-12 months, Y2 = 13-24 months, Y3 = 25-36 months, Y4+ = 37+ months, assigned from months_initiated.
  • Test: Kruskal-Wallis H-test across the four buckets, run independently per item (not a single omnibus test across all items), since the response scale is ordinal and bucket sizes are uneven (n=39, 32, 28, 3). Items reaching p < 0.05: Mental health, Conflict, Worth, Experience gap, Belonging, Values, Hazing.
  • Small-cell caution: Year 4+ has n=3. Its figures are shown for completeness only and should not be used to support any claim; no test result involving that bucket should be treated as informative.
Regret Analysis
  • Groups: Respondents who answered the regret question (77 of 102), split into Yes (n=10) and No (n=67).
  • Test: Mann-Whitney U, two-sided, run independently per item comparing Yes vs. No. Chosen over an independent-samples t-test given the ordinal scale and the roughly 7:1 imbalance between groups.
  • Multiple comparisons: No correction (e.g. Bonferroni) has been applied across the 23 per-item tests in this section, matching prior cycles. With 23 tests at alpha = 0.05, roughly 1 false positive would be expected by chance alone; treat the p = 0.08-range "marginal" items (Bullying, Rules) accordingly, and treat the two items below p = 0.05 (Belonging, Academics - major/time) as the more defensible findings.
Solutions Analysis
  • Reporting: Descriptive percentages only (No / Sort of / Yes a solution was offered). No inferential test of whether being offered a solution changed the odds of staying or of regret is run, because the intervention rate is low (11.4% received a genuine offer) and the resulting cell sizes are too small to support a reliable estimate. This mirrors the prior cycle's approach and should stay descriptive-only until the offered-solution rate rises materially.
Tooling
  • Software: Python (pandas for data handling, factor_analyzer 0.5.1 for EFA and KMO/Bartlett tests, scipy.stats for Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney U). All figures in this report are computed directly from the merged master and cohort match, not carried forward from a prior report or manually re-entered.
