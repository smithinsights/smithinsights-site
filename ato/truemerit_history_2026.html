True Merit Award: A Half-Century of Chapter Transition Dynamics

June 2026  ·  Director of Systems  ·  1968-69 through 2024-25

This report applies a binary Markov chain to ATO chapter award outcomes from 1968-69 through 2024-25 - 57 academic years of True Merit history. Each chapter is assigned one of two active states each year: True Merit (TM) or Not True Merit (NTM). A third absorbing state captures chapter closure. The model estimates year-over-year transition probabilities from observed outcomes, covering more than 7,000 individual chapter-year transitions. Chapters are coded as active based on both award history and status records from the membership system. The analysis identifies five statistically distinct eras and characterizes where the current period sits in that long-run context.

The asymmetry between the two states is fundamental. TM chapters retain their status at more than six times the rate that NTM chapters gain it. Reaching True Merit is difficult; holding it is comparatively easier. That gap between gain rate and retention rate is the defining structural feature of the award system across the full history.

Five Statistically Distinct Eras

Transition rates are not stable across 57 years. Two-sample t-tests on annual NTM gain rates identify four significant breaks in the series, producing five eras with meaningfully different chapter mobility patterns. The 2010-19 to 2020-25 boundary is the one exception - those two periods are statistically indistinguishable on both metrics, which is itself an important finding about where the system currently sits.

The Contraction Era: 1984-98

The 1984-98 period stands apart from every other era in the history. The NTM gain rate collapsed to 6.4% - roughly half the rate of any other era. TM retention fell to 52.3%, its historical low. The average number of True Merit chapters dropped from 14.4 in the Early Era to 11.6, even as the total chapter pool grew from roughly 36 to more than 85 active chapters. The share of chapters earning TM in any given year fell from approximately 40% to under 15%.

The contraction is visible in the chapter count data as well. NTM chapters nearly tripled (21.5 to 73.5 average), while TM chapters shrank slightly. The combination of a larger NTM pool and a lower gain rate meant far fewer TM outcomes system-wide, even as the number of eligible chapters grew substantially.

Structural Interpretation

The 1984-98 contraction era coincides with the period of large-scale chapter expansion and then significant chapter attrition that characterized Greek life broadly in that period. The NTM closure rate also appeared for the first time as a measurable phenomenon (2.2% annually) - closures in the Early Era were negligible. This era is the historical baseline for what a struggling award system looks like. Every subsequent era has outperformed it on both metrics.

Recovery and Modern Eras: 1999-2019

The transition from contraction to recovery is statistically sharp (p = 0.012 on NTM gain rate). From 1999 forward, the NTM gain rate returned to levels last seen in the Early Era, and TM retention climbed to the highest sustained level in the full history. The 2010-19 Modern Era extended this improvement, with the NTM gain rate reaching 15.0% - the highest of any era - while retention held at 67.0%.

The Modern Era also saw the largest absolute number of TM chapters (35.6 on average per year). This reflects both improved chapter quality and growth in the active chapter pool. The system produced more TM chapters in absolute terms during 2010-19 than in any prior period, even as the NTM pool was also growing.

The Current Era: 2020-25

The 2020-25 period is statistically continuous with 2010-19 on both key metrics. The NTM gain rate (12.1%) is below the Modern Era high of 15.0%, but the difference does not clear statistical significance (p = 0.212). TM retention (69.7%) is the highest of any era. The NTM closure rate (5.2%) is the highest in the full history, though the absolute number of closures per year remains modest.

Where We Are

The current era is a strong period by historical standards. TM retention is at its highest point ever, and the NTM gain rate - while below the 2010-19 peak - remains well above the contraction era floor. The open question is whether the NTM gain rate decline from 15.0% to 12.1% represents a genuine regression or normal year-to-year variation. With only six years in the current era, the confidence interval around that estimate is wide. More years of data will determine whether 2010-19 was a high-water mark or whether the current era can return to that level.

One structural shift is worth noting. The average number of TM chapters per year has declined from 35.6 in the Modern Era to 27.7 in the current era, while the NTM pool has grown to its largest recorded size (83.0 average). This means a smaller fraction of active chapters are earning TM annually, even with retention holding strong. If the NTM pool continues to grow while TM chapter count stays flat or declines, the system-level TM rate will compress further regardless of what individual chapter behavior looks like.

  • TM retention (69.7%): The highest sustained retention rate in the full 57-year history. Chapters that reach TM are holding it at an unprecedented rate in the current era.
  • NTM gain rate (12.1%): Above the long-run average and well above the contraction-era floor. The pipeline from NTM to TM remains healthy by historical standards.
  • NTM closure rate (5.2%): The highest closure rate in recorded history for NTM chapters. A larger and older NTM pool may be driving this - chapters that have been NTM for extended periods are more likely to close.
  • TM chapter count (avg 27.7): Down from the Modern Era peak. The total TM output per year has declined even as retention improved, because the inflow from NTM has not fully compensated for chapter closures and departures from the TM pool.
What Drives Era-Level Change

The Markov model measures transitions but does not explain them. Three classes of factors can shift an era's transition rates without changing the award criteria themselves.

Chapter pool composition. The Early Era operated with a smaller, older system where most active chapters had been established for decades. The contraction era saw rapid expansion followed by attrition. A younger or less stable chapter population produces a lower NTM gain rate simply because new chapters need time to build the programs the award recognizes.

Award administration and criteria. Changes to COS content, weighting, or scoring thresholds directly affect who reaches TM. A stricter standard reduces both the gain rate and the retention rate. A more accessible standard raises both. Without controlling for criteria changes over time, era-level shifts in transition rates are a composite of behavioral change and measurement change.

Macro conditions affecting Greek life. The contraction era (1984-98) coincides with a period of significant external pressure on fraternities nationally - insurance crises, health and safety incidents, and broad enrollment disruptions. The recovery era (1999-09) coincides with stabilization and renewed interest in Greek life. These macro conditions are likely to have influenced chapter behavior and award outcomes independent of anything the National Fraternity did programmatically.

Analytical Limit

The model cannot separate program quality improvement from favorable environmental conditions. A rising NTM gain rate in the 2010s is consistent with better chapter programs, with a more favorable chapter pool, with award criteria evolution, or with all three simultaneously. The transition rates measure outcomes, not causes. Causal claims require additional data not captured in the award history alone.

Analytical Implications

1
Monitor the NTM gain rate as a leading system health indicator Retention is a lagging measure - it tells you whether strong chapters stayed strong. The NTM gain rate is a leading measure - it tells you whether the system is producing new TM chapters from the broader pool. A sustained decline in the NTM gain rate below 10% would signal a return toward contraction-era conditions and should prompt a diagnostic review of what is blocking the pathway to TM.
2
The growing NTM pool warrants strategic attention At 83 average NTM chapters per year and a 5.2% annual closure rate, the current era is producing the most chapter closures in recorded history - in absolute terms, roughly four to five closures per year originating from the NTM pool. This is not a crisis, but it is a structural condition that compounds if the NTM pool continues to grow without a corresponding increase in the TM gain rate.
3
Use the contraction era as a calibration reference The 1984-98 period provides a documented floor for what poor system performance looks like: 6.4% NTM gain, 52.3% TM retention, and a shrinking absolute TM count despite a growing chapter pool. Every planning or evaluation conversation about chapter health metrics should reference that floor explicitly. The current era is well above it, but the distance is not infinite.
4
Revisit whether the 2010-19 gain rate was structurally achievable or a high-water mark The Modern Era's 15.0% NTM gain rate is a full 3 percentage points above the current era. If that gap persists over several more years, it suggests a structural shift - possibly the aging of the NTM pool, possibly changes in chapter programming depth, possibly award criteria effects. Understanding the mechanism behind the gap is more useful than simply tracking whether the number goes up or down.
