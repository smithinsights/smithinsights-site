The Average ATO Man

Birkman Facilitator Guide  ·  Leadership Consultant Training  ·  Prepared by Chris Smith
Basis: 1,681 men who joined since 2022 with a completed Birkman

Every leadership consultant hears the four biggest mistakes early, and they have held up since 2018 because they are not opinions. Each one sits on top of one of the strongest needs the average ATO man carries. This guide gives you the Birkman behind each mistake so consultants understand why the mistake costs them the room, then adds the one high need the four mistakes do not cover: the need that shapes how a visit should be run.

How he shows up, and what is underneath

The average ATO man presents as a man who has it handled. He is outgoing in a group, looks organized and on top of details, moves fast, and comes across direct. A new consultant who believes the surface walks in light, because the surface says this man does not need much.

His needs run high underneath it. He needs more emotional understanding, more room to think, more direction from the consultant, and more follow-through on commitments than his confident exterior suggests. The four mistakes are the four needs the surface hides. The bars below show the gap. Light blue is the behavior others see (Usual). Gold is what he needs from the people around him (Needs).

The Four Biggest Mistakes

Canonical order, unchanged. Each card pairs the mistake with the need that drives it, what the need looks like in the room, and how a consultant works with it instead of against it.

1
Paying insufficient attention to what members are feeling
Emotional Energy · Need 81 · his single highest

What is underneath: There is feeling under every report he gives. "Recruitment is down" carries frustration or embarrassment he will not lead with. His Needs score is the highest of any component, so the gap between what he says and what he feels is wide, and he expects the consultant to close it.

Do
  • Hear the feeling before moving to solutions. Let him finish.
  • Make yourself available one-on-one to hear individual concerns, not only the chapter report.
Avoid
  • Cutting him off to reach your agenda.
  • Running the visit as pure logistics and metrics.
For the room

Ask the consultants for a time a leader's real concern was sitting under the words and they missed it. This is the need that is easiest to walk past because the man in front of you is not asking you to notice it.

2
Failing to make clear exactly who is in charge
Assertiveness · Need 76

What is underneath: He needs to know where the consultant stands. Ambiguity does not read as humility, it reads as the consultant not knowing the job. He comes across direct himself, and he wants the same back.

Do
  • Take a position. Say what you recommend and why.
  • Make clear where your authority sits, and where the BOT, Greek advisor, and chapter officers carry theirs.
Avoid
  • "It is up to you" hedging on questions he brought to you for an answer.
  • Undercutting his officers, BOT, or advisor to gain standing with him.
For the room

This is the credibility mistake. It is the Birkman behind "he expects you to know what you are talking about." A consultant who hedges to seem agreeable loses him on the first hard question.

3
Pushing members to quickly make up their minds
Thought · Need 80

What is underneath: He wants to weigh it, and he worries about the wrong call. His pause is deliberation, not resistance. Read it as a stall and a consultant pushes exactly when the man needs room.

Do
  • Put the decision in front of him ahead of time. Lay out the options.
  • Give him room to sit with large calls and come back to them.
Avoid
  • Springing a decision in the room and forcing a yes.
  • Rushing the big ones: membership decisions, signature event direction, elections.
For the room

Pair this with Mistake 2. The consultant is direct about the recommendation, then patient about the timeline. Direct on the what, patient on the when.

4
Failing to follow through on commitments made to members
Incentives · Need 74

What is underneath: He reads as team-first on the surface (Usual 40), and he is tracking what he gets back for what he puts in (Needs 74). The original mistake is financial, and the need is broader. He keeps a quiet ledger on every promise made to him.

Do
  • Deliver what you commit. A resource, a follow-up, a connection, a dues rate explained.
  • Announce any incentive or expectation well in advance, and keep it visible.
Avoid
  • Quiet walk-backs and surprise reversals on anything you promised.
  • Unanticipated fees, fines, or special treatment that breaks the deal he thought he had.
For the room

Make the consultants name a small promise they could make on a visit and forget by the next one. The forgettable ones are where this need gets violated, not the big formal commitments.

Running the visit: the fifth high need

The four mistakes protect the relationship. They keep a consultant from losing him. They do not say how to run the time well, and that is where the second-highest need lives. Restlessness need is 80, one point below his top need, and none of the four mistakes touches it. As consultants move from "do not lose him" to "do this job well," this is the need that shapes the visit itself.

  • He gets bored fast and disengages. The same check-in template five visits running costs the relationship even when no mistake is tripped.
  • Vary the format. Do not run an identical agenda every time.
  • Keep the pace up. Belaboring a settled point reads as wasted time.
  • Give him something new to work on each visit, not a recycled list.
  • Momentum between visits matters as much as the visit.
The line for consultants

The four mistakes keep you from losing him. Restlessness is how you keep him moving. One protects the floor. The other sets the ceiling.

Talking Points to Leave With Consultants

1
Do not trust the surface. He looks like he has it handled. His needs run high underneath, and that gap is the whole job.
2
Hear the feeling first. Emotional Energy is his highest need. There is something under the report, and he expects you to catch it.
3
Have a point of view. Hedging reads as not knowing the job. Be direct on the recommendation.
4
Direct on the what, patient on the when. Give him the call early and the room to weigh it. The pause is deliberation.
5
Keep every promise, including the small ones. He keeps a quiet ledger. A forgotten follow-up costs more than it looks.
6
Keep it moving. Vary the visit, hold the pace, bring something new. Boredom loses him even when nothing goes wrong.
