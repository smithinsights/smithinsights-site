True Merit Transition Analysis

June 2026  ·  Director of Systems  ·  Internal Research

What separates chapters that sustain True Merit year over year from chapters that plateau into Honorable Mention or decline into Not Considered? This analysis examines year-over-year Chapter Operations Survey responses from chapters that held True Merit in one year, then traces what they were doing differently in the year before their next award decision. Three distinct operational profiles emerge.

Methodology. This analysis covers consecutive-year COS pairs across 2021-22 through 2024-25, restricted to chapters that held True Merit in year one. Because ATO does not store Honorable Mention and Not Considered status in the database, chapters that lost True Merit are divided into two proxy groups using their COS operational profile: chapters whose survey responses remained strong are classified as the Stalling group (Honorable Mention proxy), and chapters whose responses declined materially are classified as the Eroding group (Not Considered proxy). All percentages represent the share of chapters answering yes in the year prior to the award decision.

Three Transition Profiles
TM to TM
Sustaining
Chapters that won True Merit in consecutive years. 61 chapter-year pairs across three transition windows. These chapters are living their operational identity daily, not executing it seasonally.
TM to HM (proxy)
Stalling
Former TM chapters whose COS profile remained operationally strong after losing recognition. 14 chapter-year pairs. Doing most things right but no longer doing enough to earn the award.
TM to NC (proxy)
Eroding
Former TM chapters whose COS profile declined materially after losing recognition. 13 chapter-year pairs. Institutional infrastructure is weakening, not just performance slipping.

The findings below are drawn from COS responses in the year before each chapter's award decision. The question is not what they reported after losing TM -- it is what they were already doing differently while they still held it.

What Sustains True Merit Year Over Year

These are the behaviors where sustaining chapters are consistently higher than both groups that lost True Merit. The gradient from sustaining to stalling to eroding is visible in each one. None of these are award-season behaviors. They are routine practices that compound over time into an operational identity that recognition follows.

Ritual reviewed after each initiation
89%Sustaining
75%Stalling
50%Eroding
Sustaining chapters treat ritual as a living practice. After each initiation the chapter revisits what was done and how. Eroding chapters have reduced this to a one-time event -- perform, move on. The drop from 89% to 50% signals that initiation has stopped being a moment of institutional reflection and become a box checked.
Chapter eats meals together at the house
85%Sustaining
33%Stalling
33%Eroding
This is the starkest divide in the data. Sustaining chapters share meals. Both groups that lost TM stopped doing it at the same rate, regardless of whether their operational profile is otherwise strong. Shared meals are not a programming decision -- they are a brotherhood identity signal. Their absence suggests the chapter's daily life has fragmented well before the award gap became visible.
Faculty and administration invited to chapter events
74%Sustaining
55%Stalling
20%Eroding
Sustaining chapters are embedded in campus institutional life. The sharp drop at the eroding level -- only 1 in 5 chapters -- indicates these chapters have lost their relationship with the institution itself, not just with specific administrators. Stalling chapters still have some of these relationships. Eroding chapters have let them lapse.
Greek Advisor invited to a chapter meeting each year
63%Sustaining
55%Stalling
10%Eroding
Only 1 in 10 eroding chapters maintained this relationship. Stalling chapters still have it -- this is one of the cleaner dividers between the two groups that lost TM. A chapter whose Greek Advisor has not been to a chapter meeting reflects a campus relationship that has been deprioritized, which typically extends to other institutional partnerships as well.
Physical facility upkeep system in place
97%Sustaining
83%Stalling
50%Eroding
Sustaining chapters run their facility like an institution -- scheduled maintenance, cleaning systems, exterior upkeep. Eroding chapters have let this drift. A chapter that is not maintaining its physical environment is typically one where operational discipline has weakened broadly, not just in visible programming areas.
Officer notebook system in use
86%Sustaining
75%Stalling
61%Eroding
Officer notebooks are how chapters carry institutional knowledge through leadership transitions. Sustaining chapters have built continuity into the way they transfer responsibilities. Each point drop in this indicator represents a leadership transition increasingly dependent on individual memory rather than documented practice -- a fragility that compounds each year.
Thomas Arkle Clark nominee submitted
71%Sustaining
25%Stalling
17%Eroding
Sustaining chapters nominate members for the organization's highest individual recognition at more than triple the rate of chapters that lose TM. Both stalling and eroding chapters have nearly abandoned this practice. It reflects whether the chapter is actively engaged with NHQ recognition systems year-round -- identifying members who embody what the organization recognizes, not just submitting compliance paperwork.
Budget reports, H&S compliance, annual report, service records -- all complete
100%Sustaining
100%Stalling
83%Eroding
Compliance is effectively universal among sustaining and stalling chapters -- it does not separate them. But among eroding chapters it has begun to slip. A former TM chapter missing compliance submissions is not just losing an award -- it is showing the early signal pattern of a chapter beginning to withdraw from the organization. Stalling chapters do not show this pattern.
What Separates Stalling from Eroding

Among chapters that lost True Merit, the following behaviors distinguish those whose operational profile remained strong (stalling) from those whose profile declined materially (eroding). These indicators answer whether a former TM chapter is experiencing a temporary performance gap or genuine institutional deterioration. Stalling chapters have lost the award but retained the infrastructure. Eroding chapters are losing the infrastructure itself.

IFC 100% participation in activities
100%Stalling
50%Eroding
Stalling chapters have kept their IFC relationship fully intact. Eroding chapters have withdrawn from half the activities they are expected to attend. IFC disengagement is typically among the first visible signals that a chapter is pulling back from campus life more broadly.
Chapter President attends BOT meetings
82%Stalling
30%Eroding
This is the sharpest governance divider between the two groups. Stalling chapters have maintained the BOT relationship at the presidential level -- the most important connection between current chapter leadership and the alumni board. Only 3 in 10 eroding chapters have this in place. When the chapter president stops attending BOT meetings, the BOT loses its primary channel into chapter operations.
Committee structure reports to Executive Board before chapter meetings
100%Stalling
69%Eroding
Every stalling chapter has a functioning committee-to-executive reporting structure. Eroding chapters have let this slip in nearly a third of cases. When committees stop reporting to the Executive Board before chapter meetings, the chapter loses the internal communication loop that keeps leadership aligned with what is actually happening across chapter functions.
All initiated members on a committee
64%Stalling
10%Eroding
Stalling chapters still have broad member involvement in chapter operations. Eroding chapters have effectively abandoned this -- only 1 in 10 ensure every member is on a committee. When member involvement in governance collapses, chapter work concentrates in a small officer group, which creates fragility and reduces member investment in the chapter's outcomes.
Financial statements presented to initiated members
93%Stalling
58%Eroding
Stalling chapters maintain financial transparency with the membership. Eroding chapters have let this lapse in roughly 4 in 10 cases. Financial transparency with members is both a governance practice and a trust signal -- when members stop seeing the numbers, their investment in how the chapter spends money typically follows.
Formal pledging ceremony with each recruiting class
96%Stalling
61%Eroding
Stalling chapters are still running a structured, formal pledge experience. Eroding chapters have allowed this to become informal in nearly 4 in 10 cases. A pledge experience that lacks a formal ceremony reflects a broader drift away from the ritual identity practices that define ATO membership from the first day of the pledge period.
ATO Devotional book in use
82%Stalling
39%Eroding
Stalling chapters have retained their devotional identity practice even after losing TM. Eroding chapters have largely abandoned it. This connects directly to the shared meals signal in the prior section -- chapters that maintain daily brotherhood identity practices are stalling, not eroding, regardless of what the award outcome says about them.
Members enrolled in leadership development classes
64%Stalling
10%Eroding
Stalling chapters are still investing in individual member development outside the chapter. Only 1 in 10 eroding chapters does this. When a chapter stops sending members to campus or external leadership programs, it is signaling that member development has become internally focused at best and deprioritized at worst.
Parents Club in place
64%Stalling
10%Eroding
Stalling chapters have maintained their external support network. Eroding chapters have almost entirely abandoned it. A Parents Club is not a programming artifact -- it is an external constituency that provides advocacy, financial support, and institutional continuity. Its absence reflects how far a chapter's outward engagement has contracted.
News releases issued to local media
73%Stalling
10%Eroding
Stalling chapters are still telling their story externally. Only 1 in 10 eroding chapters issues any media communication. External visibility through local media reflects whether a chapter sees itself as a campus and community actor. Eroding chapters have contracted inward and stopped communicating outward.
Campus leadership program participation
100%Stalling
60%Eroding
Every stalling chapter participates in campus leadership development. Eroding chapters have withdrawn in 4 in 10 cases. Campus leadership program participation keeps the chapter networked with the institution and produces members whose development is recognized beyond the Greek system. Its absence signals the chapter is becoming self-contained.
Chapter visits or invites police over each term
82%Stalling
30%Eroding
Stalling chapters maintain proactive community relationships, including with law enforcement. Eroding chapters have let this lapse in 7 of 10 cases. This practice is a risk management signal as much as a community relations one -- chapters that build the relationship before an incident occurs are in a different position than chapters that have no relationship when one occurs.

The stalling-to-eroding transition is defined by the loss of three connected things: BOT presidential engagement, IFC community presence, and member-level governance involvement (committees, financial transparency, leadership development). Stalling chapters have not lost operational discipline -- they have lost the margin of distinctiveness that earns recognition. Eroding chapters are losing the operational discipline itself.
