Overview

This report presents findings from the Alpha Tau Omega depledge survey, covering men who joined a chapter and did not complete initiation. All 591 respondents with a valid 18-character Salesforce ID were matched to the member database. Survey responses are rated on a five-point scale (1 = did not influence, 5 = was the primary reason I left). Items marked N/A are treated as 1.

Top Three Reasons for Leaving

Three items ranked highest by mean influence score across all 591 respondents. Open-text responses were reviewed alongside the quantitative ratings to characterize the sentiment profile of each.

#1 Reason
Experience Gap
2.64
Negative sentiment
Men described being recruited on a version of membership that did not match what they encountered. Open-text responses were markedly negative in tone - they felt misled, not merely disappointed. 20.5% named this as the primary reason they left.
#2 Reason
Belonging
2.45
Negative sentiment
Men reported not feeling connected to chapter members or that they mattered within the group. Open-text sentiment was negative - language reflected rejection and invisibility rather than neutral disengagement. 15.7% named it the primary reason.
#3 Reason
Perceived Worth
2.46
Neutral sentiment
Men felt the time and financial investment no longer justified membership. Open-text sentiment was more measured - men described a rational cost-benefit conclusion rather than an emotional grievance. 15.8% named it the primary reason.

Factor Structure: Five Themes

An exploratory factor analysis was conducted on all 23 influence items (N=591). Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin = 0.831, Bartlett's test p < .001. Five factors retained by Kaiser criterion, explaining 56.4% of total variance. Varimax rotation. Each factor is a cluster of items that co-occur - men who cited one item in a cluster tended to cite others in the same cluster.

Factor 1
Organizational Fit & Culture
alpha = 0.849  ·  28.3% of variance  ·  7 items
Experience gapConflictWorth BelongingOrganizational changeValuesRules
Dominant factor, accounting for more variance than the other four combined. These seven items share one thread: the organization did not match who the man was or what he was led to expect. Experience gap, worth, and belonging load most strongly. Men who cited one reliably cited others. This factor carries the only statistically significant downward trend across cohorts (rho = -0.103, p = .018).
Factor 3
Toxic Environment
alpha = 0.820  ·  7.7% variance  ·  6 items
HazingBullyingAlcohol/substance Mental healthAcademics (grades)Mentorship
Safety and climate cluster. Grades suffering loads here rather than with the Academic factor - in this cluster, academic decline is a downstream effect of a harmful environment. Dropped sharply after the 2015-16 cohort and has remained stable since.
Factor 2
Academic & Time Demands
alpha = 0.694  ·  8.8% variance  ·  3 items
Time commitmentAcademics (major/balance)Financial (own pocket)
External pressure items competing with membership for time and money. No significant trend over time. The one factor more prevalent when chapters did offer solutions prior to departure - chapters intervene when the reason is concrete and external.
Factor 4
Financial Burden
alpha = 1.000  ·  6.3% variance  ·  2 items
Financial (parent-dependent)Financial (bill accumulated)
Parent-funded or accumulated-debt financial failure - distinct from self-funded pressure in F2. Slight uptick in 2020-21 cohort, consistent with COVID-era financial disruption, returning to near zero by 2025-26.
Factor 5
Logistical & Personal Mismatch
alpha = 0.380  ·  5.1% variance  ·  3 items
Housing requirementFound what I needed earlyReligious components
Weak factor with low reliability. These items share some statistical variance but do not form a strong construct. Better understood as circumstantial departures tied to specific requirements. Interpret each item independently.

The 2015-16 cohort is elevated on all three factors. Factor 3 dropped sharply after that cohort and stabilized. Removal Process

Among respondents removed by the chapter, 40.4% reported they were not told why they were asked to leave - more than academic failure (5.0%), behavioral conduct (11.2%), connection issues (11.8%), or member conflict (12.4%). Membership education chairmen and executive board members are the most frequent removal communicators; board of trustees involvement was reported by 0.6% of removed respondents.
