Why Men Stay

Facilitator Guide  ·  60-Minute Connection Segment  ·  Chapter Leaders

This segment opens with what the data shows about why men leave ATO, then runs three short activities to surface why men stay. You are walking the room toward two or three concrete things the National Fraternity can begin tracking to tell who is likely to stay and who is at risk. The activities do double duty: they strengthen brotherhood by getting men to say out loud why they are committed. Remember who is in the room. These are elected, older members, so their answers reflect a leader who already made it through the risky window. Frame their input as expert testimony from survivors, not as a survey of the average member.

Before You Start
Materials
  • Printed Anchor Map worksheet, one per man (grid on the last page of this guide).
  • Blank index cards or half-sheets for The Breaking Point, one per man, plus pens.
  • A flip chart or whiteboard for sorting moment types and posting the final vote.
  • The opening data slide showing departure timing and the pledge-brother contagion figure.
Room setup
  • Tables of 4 to 6. Men need small groups they can talk across for Activities 1 and 2.
  • Keep the anonymous collection point visible for Activity 3 so men trust it is truly unattributed.
Facilitator note

The three captured measures are the point. If you run short on time, protect the capture step at the end of each activity over the discussion. A great conversation with nothing written down leaves us nothing to track.

Opening · What We Know About Men Who Leave

10 minutes. Set the stakes before you ask them to open up. Keep it short and let the numbers land. You are earning the right to run the activities that follow.

Points to make
  • Most departures land early. The risk is front-loaded, and the window stays open through roughly the two-year mark.
  • Men leave in clusters. When one man resigns, his pledge brothers become about 2.5 times more likely to follow.
  • The first resignation in a pledge class is a leading indicator, not an isolated loss.
We know a fair amount about why men leave. Today I want your help with the other side of that question. You are the men who stayed. I want to know what held you, so we can build more of it on purpose.
Activity 1
The Anchor Map
15 min
What it is

A quick personal exercise where each man identifies the brothers who hold him in the chapter, marks how each is tied to him by pledge class, and marks whether he has ever lived with him.

Why it is here

Men tend to leave in clusters, especially when close friends from their own pledge class leave first, and shared living arrangements make those ties tighter and the exposure more concentrated. This surfaces each man's "if they left, I would think about leaving too" people, and shows how much of his anchoring is stacked into one group he also lives or lived with.

How to run it
  • 12 minHand out the worksheet. Explain the three marks: the brother's initials, whether he is from the same pledge class, older, or younger, and whether they have ever lived together.
  • 25 minMen work privately. Each writes the 3 to 5 brothers who anchor him and completes all three marks for each.
  • 36 minIn small groups, men compare three things out loud: how many anchors they have, how many sit in a single pledge group, and how many they have lived with.
  • 42 minCapture the three numbers per man before moving on. See the capture note below.
Write the men who, if they walked away from ATO, would make you seriously think about walking too. Not your favorite guys. The ones whose leaving would shake you.

The most exposed man has few anchors, all from one group, and all men he has lived with. The trackable version for every member becomes anchor count, cluster concentration, and lived-with share.

Activity 2
The Moment
17 min
What it is

Storytelling about the moment ATO stopped being something a man did and became part of who he is.

Why it is here

The riskiest stretch runs to about the two-year mark. We want to know what creates real commitment before then, so chapters can make those moments happen on purpose instead of hoping they occur.

How to run it
  • 12 minFrame the prompt. This is one specific moment, not a general feeling. A scene with a time and a place.
  • 28 minIn pairs, each man tells the story of the exact moment he knew he was staying. Give each man roughly four minutes.
  • 35 minPairs name the type of moment to their table. As a table, group the types. Post recurring types on the flip chart.
  • 42 minCapture the sorted list of moment types across the room.
Tell me the moment you knew you were in for good. Not the day you signed. The specific moment it stopped being a thing you joined and became part of who you are.
Listen for these types

A brother showing up in a personal crisis. Being handed a real role with real responsibility. A retreat or a ritual moment. Getting carried through a low point by the chapter. These are the moments we will try to engineer earlier for new members.

What we keep

The list of what actually turns a man into a lifelong member. The trackable version is simple: has each new member had his moment yet, and by when.

Activity 3
The Breaking Point
12 min
What it is

An honest, anonymous look at what would have had to break for each man to leave.

Why it is here

It pressure-tests our list of reasons men leave from the inside, using the men who made it through the risky window. They know which threads were actually load-bearing because they nearly pulled on them.

How to run it
  • 12 minHand out cards. Stress that this is anonymous and collected blind. No names, no identifying detail.
  • 23 minEach man privately writes the one thing that, if it disappeared, would have made him leave.
  • 31 minCollect all cards into the visible collection point and shuffle.
  • 44 minRead cards aloud, group the repeats on the board, then have the room vote on which matter most.
  • 52 minCapture the short ranked list of load-bearing things.
Write the one thing that, if it vanished tomorrow, would have made you walk. One thing. The load-bearing wall. No name on the card.
What we keep

The short list of load-bearing things that hold men in. For any member we can then ask whether his is present or missing, which gives us an early warning checklist.

Close · Name the Takeaways

6 minutes. Hand the room the pen. Ask them to name the two or three things they are carrying out of this session. Write them where everyone can see. This is what turns an exercise into a commitment, and it gives you the room's own words for the follow-up.

In your own words, what are the two or three things you are taking out of this room today? Not what I said. What you landed on.
The Three Things We Walk Out Tracking

Everything above rolls up to measures the National Fraternity can begin asking of every member.

  • Anchor exposure: anchor count, cluster concentration, and lived-with share per man. Flags who is most exposed if a single group leaves.
  • The moment: has each new member had his commitment moment, and by when. Flags who is still uncommitted inside the risk window.
  • The load-bearing wall: is each man's one essential thing present or missing. An early warning checklist built from the men who survived.
