ATO has maintained member records since 1865. That record tells us not just who joined, but when they left, why they left, and how those patterns have changed across every cohort since tracking began. No single chapter can hold this view, the National Fraternity can.

What’s Going On With Greek Life?
The claim: Greek organizations are shrinking and will continue to decline. The data: Both ATO and NPC membership have rebounded since 2020-21. The challenges are real but the trajectory is not terminal.
ATO NPC NPC on right axis for membership & chapter count

Chapter Count

Since 2021-22, ATO membership and average chapter size have both recovered. Chapter count has continued to decline, from 143 to 125. The organization is serving more members per chapter than at any point since 2017-18, but through fewer chapters. Growth now depends almost entirely on what happens inside existing chapters.

ATO’s Single-Gender Status Matters
Support grew from 3.75 in 2021 to 4.19 in 2025. Nearly 58% strongly agree in 2025.
Risky Behavior Is Not Seen as a One-Person Problem
Members consistently view risky behavior as a chapter-level issue. Averages held between 3.37 and 3.51 across five years.
Campus Freedom to Speak
Freedom to speak on campus improved from 3.56 in 2021 to 3.99 in 2025. Strong disagreement dropped from 16% to 2%.
ATO members support the single-gender model and that support is growing. They see risky behavior as something the whole chapter owns, not just the person doing it. And they increasingly feel free to speak openly on campus.

What COVID Did to a Generation of Members
Fall cohort Spring cohort
Fall 2018 and Spring 2019 cohorts score highest. Spring 2020 scores lowest. The effect follows pledge class timing, not survey year.
Members feel more free to speak inside their chapter than anywhere else on campus. Men who pledged during the COVID period experienced something genuinely different, and the data shows it. What a member goes through during their pledge class shapes how they experience the organization long after.

Segment 2  ·  Chapter Data

What We Know
About Our
Chapters

Chapter data is the operational record of ATO in action, what chapters are doing, how they are being led, and how they measure against the standards the National Fraternity holds. No individual chapter sees the full picture. The data tells us where to focus and where to intervene before a problem becomes a crisis.
Segment 2  ·  Chapter Data
The Chapter Visit
Every visit has a paperwork component and your organization depends on you completing it accurately. But the paperwork captures what was true on one day. What you observe on a visit is a data point no form can collect - and over time, your observations become some of the most valuable intelligence the National Fraternity has about chapter health.

Segment 3  ·  Higher Education
Higher Education Enrollment Trends
Left axis: Total enrollment
Actual (2018-19 to 2024-25) Projected (CDC birth cohorts)
Right axis: Degree completers
Male completers Female completers
Enrollment peaks at 2.47M in 2025-26 then declines. Female completers overtake male around 2029-30.
Enrollment is near its peak. The 2008-2010 birth cohort cliff will cut the student population by roughly 180,000 between 2025-26 and 2028-29. Men graduate at lower rates than women in every year on record. Female degree completers are projected to overtake male completers in absolute numbers by 2029-30, despite women being the smaller enrolled group. Fewer men will be earning degrees in the years ahead.
n=805 institutions, 4-year public & private nonprofit. Degree completers = enrolled × 6-year graduation rate (men 62.0%, women 68.7%). Sources: IPEDS EF-C; CDC National Vital Statistics.
