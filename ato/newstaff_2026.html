Section 1  ·  New Staff Training
The Environment We Operate In
Higher education trends
  • Overall college enrollment has declined and plateaued in many regions
  • Male enrollment has declined faster than female enrollment
  • Fewer college-aged men exist today compared to prior decades
  • Competition for students' time and organizational attention is higher than ever
What this means for ATO
  • Recruitment is structurally harder than it was 20 years ago
  • Growth does not happen automatically even in healthy chapters
  • Organizational challenges are often higher education challenges first
The headwinds ATO faces are not unique to fraternities. They are part of a larger shift in American higher education.

Hot Topic Awareness  ·  Sports Betting
Sports Betting: Who Bets and What They See
How often members bet
  • Most members who bet do so a few times a year or about once a week
  • A meaningful group bets multiple times a week
  • Heavy bettors (76-100% frequency) are a small but persistent segment in both 2024 and 2025
Betting behavior did not disappear between 2024 and 2025. The distribution shifted but the pattern held.
What bettors believe about their chapter
  • Members who bet multiple times a week believe 47-53% of their chapter members also bet regularly
  • Members who bet rarely believe far fewer chapter members bet
  • Heavy bettors consistently overestimate chapter engagement compared to light bettors
  • This perception gap matters: normalized environments encourage continued behavior
The more frequently a member bets, the more he believes his chapter shares that behavior. Heavy bettors are operating inside a perceived norm that lighter bettors do not recognize.

Section 4  ·  Your Role
Every Staff Member Is a Researcher
You collect information when you
  • Visit a chapter and observe how it operates
  • Have a conversation with a chapter president
  • Build a relationship with a campus administrator
  • Review and verify roster accuracy
  • Encourage members to complete surveys
  • Record your observations in visit reports
  • Follow up on a concern that a member raised
What that information becomes
  • Expansion decisions
  • Recruitment coaching and support
  • Chapter health identification
  • Program design and iteration
  • Risk identification before it becomes a crisis
  • Resource allocation across the Fraternity
The quality of future ATO decisions depends directly on the quality of information gathered today. That gathering happens through people, not systems.
